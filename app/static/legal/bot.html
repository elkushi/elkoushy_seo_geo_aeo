About our crawler

For website owners and administrators who see SEO-GEO-AEO-Audit in their logs.

In short: our crawler only visits your website when a user of this service requests an audit of it. It reads at most about 60 pages, with no more than 4 requests at a time, respects your robots.txt, and can be blocked with two lines in robots.txt. It also sends up to 4 homepage requests with AI-crawler user agents to test firewall access (details below).

How to recognise it

The main crawler identifies itself as SEO-GEO-AEO-Audit/1.0 (+[BOT_INFO_URL]), appended to a browser-like user agent string. The link points to this page. The full string is:

Mozilla/5.0 (X11; Linux x86_64) AppleWebKit/537.36 (KHTML, like Gecko) Chrome/130.0 Safari/537.36 SEO-GEO-AEO-Audit/1.0 (+[BOT_INFO_URL, e.g. https://example.com/bot])

Requests come from [SERVER IP ADDRESS(ES)], hosted by Hetzner Online GmbH in the EU.

When it runs

  • Only when a signed-in user asks for an audit of your website. There is no continuous or scheduled crawling.
  • Each user has a small monthly limit, and the same site cannot be re-audited repeatedly in a short time.
  • We do not build a search index, and we do not use your content to train AI models. The content is used only to create the report for the user who requested it.

What it fetches

  • Your homepage, /robots.txt, your XML sitemaps (up to 8) and /llms.txt / /llms-full.txt if they exist.
  • Then up to about 60 further pages (the user chooses how many, typically 10–25), found through links on your homepage and in your sitemap. Images, scripts, stylesheets, PDFs and other files are skipped, as are login, account, cart and checkout pages.
  • At most 4 requests in parallel. Temporary errors are retried at most twice, with a pause in between.
  • Only public addresses: the crawler refuses to connect to private or internal networks.

AI crawler access test

Besides the main crawler, each audit makes up to 4 single requests to your homepage using the user agents of AI crawlers: OAI-SearchBot, PerplexityBot, ClaudeBot and GPTBot — one request per user agent, repeated once only if it times out. Their only purpose is to check whether your firewall or CDN (for example a “block AI bots” setting) turns AI crawlers away, which robots.txt alone does not show. No other pages are requested this way.

These requests come from our server ([SERVER IP ADDRESS(ES)]), not from OpenAI, Perplexity or Anthropic, so you may see those bot names from our IP address in your logs. The report shown to the user labels the results as an audit test, not as real visits by those crawlers.

[ONLY IF CORE WEB VITALS IS ENABLED] If the user includes Core Web Vitals, Google's PageSpeed Insights service also loads your homepage from Google's own infrastructure.

robots.txt

The crawler follows the rules in your robots.txt for the group User-agent: SEO-GEO-AEO-Audit or, if there is none, for User-agent: * (RFC 9309 semantics: the longest matching rule wins). Pages you disallow are not crawled. If your robots.txt has a User-agent: SEO-GEO-AEO-Audit group that disallows your site, the audit stops after reading robots.txt: no other page is requested. Under a general User-agent: * block, the homepage, sitemaps and llms.txt are still read once per requested audit, so the report can tell the site owner that search engines are blocked.

How to block it

To stop our crawler from visiting your pages, add this to your robots.txt:

User-agent: SEO-GEO-AEO-Audit
Disallow: /

To block only part of your site, use a more specific path, for example Disallow: /private/. You can also block our IP address [SERVER IP ADDRESS(ES)] in your firewall, or ask us to exclude your domain completely — see below.
